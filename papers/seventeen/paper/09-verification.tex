\section{Verification}\label{sec:verification}

All programs, certificates and saved search records are public. The
repository is \url{https://github.com/brandonlign/quantum}, and the
full search-record database is archived on Zenodo
(\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23874369}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.23874369}
and \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23904905}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.23904905}).
The file \pathcode{REPRODUCING.md} lists the commands to run.

\emph{Standalone checks.} The programs in \pathcode{verification/}
recheck the six certificates of Proposition~\ref{prop:17-enumerator}
and its ordinary identity, a further certificate excluding minimum
stabilizer weight at least nine, and thirteen local weight-count
certificates for the profile restrictions of the mixed rank-two types.
They also check the \(\qc{17,3,5}\) construction by complete
centralizer enumeration, and recheck the local classifications of
Section~\ref{sec:local-classifications}.

\emph{Search records.} A separate program checks that every
seventeen-qubit search part finished and that every shortened-code class
was covered, including the 32,747 classes and 2,419,796 search records of
the three largest rank-one kernel cases. By default it replays the full
search on eight complete classes. The command
{\small\texttt{verify.py --{}deep}} reconstructs the aggregation of every saved
record. The searches were split between two implementations, one on a
Mac and one on Linux.

\emph{Implementation tests.} Known \(\qc{5,1,3}\), \(\qc{16,4,5}\),
and \(\qc{16,1,6}\) codes provide positive tests.
Seventeen-coordinate \(\qc{17,3,3}\) examples additionally test
prescribed local kernels, shortened-code kernels, and singular Gram
matrices. Their stabilizers and centralizers are constructed explicitly;
ranks, commutation, and minimum distance are checked by enumeration.
The complementary-image algorithm is compared with direct enumeration
on small examples, including assignments with nonzero diagonal entries.
For the mixed rank-two cases, these comparisons cover 189,860 tuples
in 1,804 fixed-tuple and distance-threshold instances. Separate C
implementations are compared on common inputs. Tests with deliberately
incomplete or inconsistent records check that these cannot be accepted
as complete exclusions.
