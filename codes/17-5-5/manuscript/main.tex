\title{An explicit optimal binary stabilizer code with parameters $[[17,5,5]]$}
\author{Brandon Li}
\date{November 2026}

\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit pure binary stabilizer code encoding five qubits into seventeen physical qubits with minimum distance five. Its stabilizer has minimum nonzero weight eight. We provide all twelve generators, a short certificate consisting of 1,276 distinct error syndromes, the complete stabilizer and normalizer weight distributions, and a self-contained rational MacWilliams certificate showing that distance six is impossible at these parameters. The code is therefore distance-optimal among binary stabilizer codes with length seventeen and dimension $2^5$. A reduced-projector purity certificate proves that it is not equivalent to any $\F_4$-linear stabilizer code under arbitrary products of single-qubit unitaries and coordinate permutations. The stabilizer is also the binary span of one thirty-element weight-eight orbit under an explicit $A_5$ action. All claims can be checked using small, dependency-free programs without reproducing the discovery search.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction and scope}
The stabilizer formalism translates quantum-code construction into the study of trace-self-orthogonal additive quaternary codes, or equivalently isotropic binary symplectic subspaces \cite{crss}. The distinction between additive and quaternary-linear codes matters: restricting a search to $\F_4$-linear codes can omit valid binary stabilizer codes.

The Grassl table entry for binary $[[17,5]]$ codes, accessed on 10 November 2026, lists lower and upper distance bounds four and five \cite{grassl}. Its displayed construction is a $[[17,5,4]]$ code. The present code attains the displayed upper bound.

Our principal contribution is the explicit code and its independently checkable certificate. The upper-bound method is classical MacWilliams linear programming \cite{sl}; our exact rational identity makes its specialization transparent. We also give short obstructions to equivalence to a quaternary-linear code, including an obstruction under arbitrary local unitaries. The latter uses the established framework of local invariants of quantum codes \cite{rainsinv,localinv}.

Ong's group-algebra construction \cite{ong}, Example~5.8, gives a $[[17,5,4]]$ code. Its stated distance is four, so that example does not supply the distance-five construction considered here.

Recent work by Girard \cite{girard} also uses explicit stabilizer constructions and independently checkable distance certificates. The direct constructions described in that work have lengths 53--254, rather than seventeen. The propagation rules stated in its Section IV increase length or decrease dimension at fixed length, so its reported propagated improvements do not reach length seventeen.

\section{Conventions}
Let $V=\F_2^{17}\oplus\F_2^{17}$, with vectors written $(x\mid z)$. Its symplectic form and Pauli weight are
\[
 \langle(x\mid z),(x'\mid z')\rangle=x\cdot z'+z\cdot x',\qquad
 \wt(x\mid z)=|\{i:(x_i,z_i)\ne(0,0)\}|.
\]
We identify $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$ with $I,X,Z,Y$, respectively, ignoring global phase. A rank-$12$ isotropic subspace $S\subset V$ determines a stabilizer code of dimension $2^{17-12}=32$. Its distance is the least weight in $S^\perp\setminus S$. If $S^\perp$ has minimum nonzero weight five, the code is pure of distance at least five.

All displayed generators are Hermitian Pauli operators with positive signs. Their binary independence ensures that no nonempty product has zero binary label; in particular the generated group cannot contain $-I$. The common $+1$ eigenspace therefore has dimension 32. Other consistent sign choices do not alter the distance arguments.

Here is a short, independently reproducible finite verification of Theorem~\ref{thm:code}. Pairwise symplectic products of the rows vanish. Forming the binary span by successive doubling gives 4,096 distinct vectors, hence rank twelve. Its weight distribution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 A(t)=1+30t^8+720t^{10}+1344t^{12}+1680t^{14}+321t^{16}.
\end{equation}
For a Pauli label $e$, define the twelve-bit syndrome
$\sigma(e)=(\langle e,s_1\rangle,\ldots,\langle e,s_{12}\rangle)$.
There are exactly
\[
 1+3\binom{17}{1}+9\binom{17}{2}=1276
\]
labels of weight at most two, and direct evaluation gives 1,276 distinct syndromes.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:syndrome}
If all Pauli labels of weight at most two have distinct syndromes, then $S^\perp$ contains no nonzero label of weight at most four.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Any label $e$ of weight at most four is the sum of two labels $a,b$ of weight at most two, obtained by partitioning its support. If $e\ne0$, then $a\ne b$. If $e\in S^\perp$, then $\sigma(a)=\sigma(b)$, contradicting injectivity.
\end{proof}
The integer $269748239$ represents a weight-five label with zero syndrome. It is not in $S$, by~\eqref{eq:A}. This proves exact distance five. The supplementary program \texttt{verify\_short.py} implements precisely this certificate using tuples of binary pairs and the printed Pauli strings, independently of the integer representation used by the longer verifier.

Separately, it enumerates physical errors of weights one through five; the numbers tested are 51, 1,224, 18,360, 192,780, and 1,503,684, with zero-syndrome counts 0, 0, 0, 0, and 420. These checks are redundant rather than additional hypotheses.

\section{An exact optimality certificate}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:optimal}
Every binary stabilizer code with length seventeen and dimension $2^5$ has distance at most five, including degenerate codes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $A_i$ denote its stabilizer weight counts. Then $A_0=1$, $\sum_iA_i=4096$, and $A_i\ge0$. Write the quaternary Krawtchouk polynomials as
\[
 K_j(i)=\sum_{a=0}^j(-1)^a3^{j-a}\binom ia\binom{17-i}{j-a},
\]
with out-of-range binomial coefficients interpreted as zero. MacWilliams duality gives $4096B_j=\sum_iK_j(i)A_i$. Distance at least six implies $B_j=A_j$ for $1\le j\le5$, even when low-weight stabilizers exist. Consequently
\[
 D_j=\sum_{i=0}^{17}K_j(i)A_i-4096A_j=0\quad(1\le j\le5).
\]
Take the linear combination of $A_0=1$, $\sum_iA_i=4096$, and these five equalities with respective multipliers
\[
 -\frac{1001}{201},\quad\frac{25}{25728},\quad
 \frac{101}{437376},\quad\frac{197}{1749504},\quad
 \frac{19}{583168},\quad\frac5{583168},\quad\frac1{583168}.
\]
After simplification, the resulting equality is
\begin{align*}
 \frac1{3417}(&8000A_1+5552A_2+3856A_3+2337A_4+1272A_5\\
 &+616A_6+252A_7+81A_8+16A_9+A_{12}+4A_{15}+9A_{16})=-1.
\end{align*}
Every term on the left is nonnegative, a contradiction. All arithmetic is rational; no floating-point optimization or assumed purity enters the certificate.
\end{proof}
Combining Theorems~\ref{thm:code} and~\ref{thm:optimal} determines the optimal stabilizer distance at these fixed parameters. The argument does not claim optimality among arbitrary nonadditive quantum codes.

\section{Symmetry and genuinely additive structure}
\subsection{A compact alternating-group construction}
The code has an equivalent presentation generated from a single Pauli word. After a coordinate permutation and local Clifford relabeling, use coordinates zero through sixteen and define
\begin{align*}
 a&=(0\ 10)(1\ 9)(2\ 11)(3\ 6)(4\ 8)(5\ 7)(13\ 14)(15\ 16),\\
 b&=(0\ 5\ 9)(1\ 11\ 7)(2\ 6\ 3)(4\ 8\ 10)(12\ 13\ 15).
\end{align*}
The action of $a$ is purely this coordinate permutation. The action of $b$ additionally rotates the Pauli labels at its two fixed coordinates:
\[
 14:\ X\mapsto Z\mapsto Y\mapsto X,\qquad
 16:\ X\mapsto Y\mapsto Z\mapsto X.
\]
All other local label maps are the identity. Thus only two nontrivial local rotations are needed.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:a5}
These binary symplectic monomial maps generate a subgroup isomorphic to $A_5$. The binary span $S'$ of the orbit of
\[
 v'=\texttt{IIZXYIYXIZIIZYIII}
\]
is locally Clifford equivalent, allowing a coordinate permutation, to the stabilizer $S$ in Table~\ref{tab:rows}. The orbit has thirty weight-eight words and spans a rank-twelve isotropic subspace.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Direct closure on the 34 binary coordinate basis vectors gives sixty transformations, with $a^2=b^3=(ab)^5=1$. Restriction to the five-site orbit $\{12,13,14,15,16\}$ gives sixty distinct even permutations, identifying the subgroup as $A_5$. Applying the group to $v'$ produces thirty distinct, pairwise commuting weight-eight words. Binary elimination gives rank twelve. The explicit invertible coordinate change in \texttt{NORMAL\_FORM.json} maps all 4,096 words of $S'$ onto those of $S$, and conjugates the two actions to the original stabilizer symmetries.

Independently of this equivalence certificate, \texttt{verify\_standalone.py} constructs $S'$ from just the displayed cycles, rotations and seed. Its 1,276 errors of weight at most two have distinct syndromes, and the integer label $1327119$ is a weight-five word in $(S')^\perp\setminus S'$. This gives a self-contained distance-five check for the compact construction.
\end{proof}
The coordinate orbits have sizes twelve and five. This is a binary monomial symmetry and a finite orbit construction; no classification of the full automorphism group or implementation of a signed transversal logical gate is asserted. The original twelve-row presentation remains the convention for the certificates below.

\subsection{A direct local-Clifford obstruction}

The displayed stabilizer is invariant under the following order-three symplectic monomial map $g$. On sites zero through fourteen, send site $3j+r$ to $3j+(r+1\bmod 3)$ for $0\le j<5$ and $0\le r<3$. On each of sites fifteen and sixteen, apply $(x,z)\mapsto(z,x+z)$. Checking the images of the twelve generators against their span proves invariance. This symmetry is a property of the binary stabilizer; no claim about a signed transversal logical gate is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Odd-overlap obstruction]\label{lem:overlap}
Suppose $s\in S$ and $d\in S^\perp$ have equal local nonzero labels wherever both are nonzero, and the number of those sites is odd. Then $S$ is not locally Clifford equivalent, even allowing coordinate permutations, to a $\F_4$-linear stabilizer.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every order-three element of $\mathrm{Sp}(2,2)$ cycles the three nonzero labels. Thus for any product $h$ of one such map at every site, $\langle h(s),d\rangle$ receives contribution one at each site where both labels are nonzero, and zero elsewhere. Its value is one, so $h(s)\notin S$. On the other hand, a $\F_4$-linear additive code is invariant under multiplication by a primitive element of $\F_4$ at every coordinate. Conjugating this action by any local Clifford equivalence gives just such an $h$ preserving $S$. Coordinate permutations merely relabel the local factors. This is a contradiction.
\end{proof}
For this code take $s$ to be row eight in Table~\ref{tab:rows}, and take
\[
 d=\texttt{IIIIIZYIIIIIZZZZI},
 \qquad\text{integer representation }8065646656.
\]
The verifier checks $d\in S^\perp$. There are exactly three sites with nonzero labels in both $s$ and $d$, and their labels agree at each. Lemma~\ref{lem:overlap} proves that the constructed code is not locally Clifford equivalent to a $\F_4$-linear stabilizer. This is stronger than failure of invariance under one particular choice of quaternary scalar multiplication. A stronger statement, including non-Clifford local unitaries, follows from a different certificate below.


\subsection{An obstruction under arbitrary local unitaries}
For any stabilizer code, let $P$ be the orthogonal projector onto its code space and let $\rho=P/\operatorname{Tr}P$ be the normalized projector. If $\mathcal S$ denotes the signed Pauli stabilizer, then
\[
 \rho=2^{-n}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}s.
\]
For a set $T$ of physical sites, put $S_T=\{s\in S:\operatorname{supp}(s)\subseteq T\}$. Partial trace removes every Pauli with support outside $T$. Orthogonality of distinct Pauli operators therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:purity}
 2^{|T|}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_T^2)=|S_T|,
 \qquad \rho_T=\operatorname{Tr}_{T^c}\rho.
\end{equation}
Signs do not affect this identity. If two code spaces are related by a product of single-qubit unitaries, their normalized projectors transform by the same conjugation, and their reduced purities agree for every $T$. Permutations simply relabel these subsets.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:LU}
The code in Theorem~\ref{thm:code} is not equivalent to a $\F_4$-linear stabilizer code under any product of single-qubit unitaries, even allowing coordinate permutations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a $\F_4$-linear stabilizer, every shortened space $S_T$ is itself a $\F_4$-linear space. Hence $|S_T|=4^r$ for some integer $r\ge0$. For our code choose
\[
 T=\{2,3,4,6,7,9,10,12\},\qquad |T|=8.
\]
Direct enumeration of its 4,096 stabilizer labels gives
\[
 S_T=\{0,221778000\},\qquad
 221778000=\texttt{IIZZYIXZIZZIXIIII}.
\]
Consequently $2^8\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_T^2)=2$, which is not a power of four. Equation~\eqref{eq:purity} and local-unitary invariance give the contradiction. This is a statement about code subspaces and their mixed normalized projectors; it does not require selecting a particular encoded pure state.
\end{proof}
The independent script \texttt{verify\_local\_unitary.py} verifies the exact shortened space. The proof does not rely on a general assertion that local-unitary and local-Clifford equivalence coincide.

\section{Reproducibility}
The verification folder contains machine-readable integer rows, printed Pauli strings, a short independent certificate checker, a full enumerator checker, a manuscript consistency checker, a reduced-purity certificate checker, an alternating-group orbit constructor, and the observed verification output. All checkers use Python's standard library only. Run \texttt{python3 verify\_short.py} and \texttt{python3 verify.py} from that folder. The latter checks the optimality identity using exact rational arithmetic. Run \texttt{python3 verify\_local\_unitary.py} for the shortened-space certificate and \texttt{python3 check\_manuscript.py} for consistency between the printed and machine-readable generators.

The short checker has three substantive tasks: construct a 4,096-element span, check 1,276 radius-two syndromes, and verify a weight-five logical witness. These are sufficient to reproduce the existence and distance claim without a large search. The full normalizer enumeration is provided to guard against implementation mistakes, not to make the proof depend on an opaque solver.

The code arose in a search preserving the order-three symmetry above. That discovery history is not part of the logical certificate: the explicit rows and their verification stand on their own.
